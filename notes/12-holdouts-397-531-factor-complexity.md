# Holdouts 397 and 531 - Factor Complexity 📊

Follow-up to `10-holdouts-397-531-shape-explosion.md`. Yesterday's finding was that 397 and 531 are shape-explosive (~95-100% distinct macro-shapes per loop). Today's question: is the irregular-side **word** Sturmian-like, substitutive, periodic, or genuinely chaotic?

**Result: both have *bounded factor complexity*, and 531 additionally has *bounded word length*.** Neither matches Sturmian (which would have unbounded c(n) ~ n+1). 531 is the closest to a finite-state-counter automaton candidate; 397 looks substitutive.

## Setup

Tool: `tools/sandbox/bb33_complexity.py` and `bb33_complexity_v2.py`.

For each holdout:
- Run Quick_Sim with `--verbose-simulator --print-loops 1 --recursive --block-size 2` to capture every macro-step config.
- Extract the "irregular side" block-label sequence as a word.
  - 397: **left** side (the growing block is on the right).
  - 531: **right** side (the growing block is on the left).
- Compute factor complexity `c(n)` = number of distinct length-n contiguous subwords.
- Statistics across ALL ~200k snapshots, not just the final.

## Headline result (200k macro-loops)

### Holdout 397

| n | min c(n) | median | p99 | **max** |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 3 | 1 | 13 | 18 | **20** |
| 5 | 1 | 16 | 21 | **22** |
| 8 | 1 | 16 | 21 | **22** |
| 12 | 1 | 16 | 20 | **22** |

- Word length: min 0, max 328, mean 115.5 (linear growth with sweeps).
- Alphabet: 5 symbols (`01`, `11`, `12`, `21`, `22`).
- **Max c(n) across the entire 200k-snapshot trace is bounded by 22 for ANY n ≥ 5.**

### Holdout 531

| n | min c(n) | median | p99 | **max** |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 3 | 1 | 12 | 17 | **21** |
| 5 | 1 | 11 | 17 | **20** |
| 8 | 1 | 8 | 15 | **17** |
| 12 | 1 | 5 | 11 | **13** |

- Word length: min 0, **max 24** (bounded!), mean 15.0.
- Alphabet: 6 symbols.

## Interpretation

### 397 - bounded-complexity substitutive candidate

Linear word-length growth + bounded factor complexity ≤ 22 (for any factor length n) is the signature of a **substitutive sequence** (e.g., Thue-Morse, Fibonacci word, Rudin-Shapiro) or of a **quasi-periodic word**.

A truly random 5-letter word of length 328 would have c(8) ≈ min(5^8, 321) = 321, not 22. A periodic word with period p has c(n) ≤ p for all n; 397's bound of 22 is consistent with an *effective period* around 20.

**Hypothesis (~50% confidence)**: 397's left-side word is generated by a finite-state substitution applied repeatedly. That is, there's a small set of macro-words and a substitution rule that maps each macro-word to a longer macro-word at the next "level."

Testing would require: identify a candidate substitution by inspecting consecutive snapshots, check whether one word is a substitution of the previous.

### 531 - bounded-length, bounded-complexity, finite-state candidate

The right-side word for 531 has **bounded length (max 24)** AND bounded factor complexity. That's a much stronger structure.

The natural framing: 531's full state at macro-step `t` decomposes as
- A counter `N` (the `12^N` block on the left).
- A "context word" `w ∈ Σ*` on the right with `|w| ≤ 24`.

Each macro step maps `(w, N) → (w', N')`. Since `w` is bounded length over alphabet of 6, there are at most `6^24 ≈ 5 × 10^18` possible `w` values - finite. **If the set of reachable `w` is much smaller (we see ~60k distinct full words in 200k snapshots, so ≤ a few thousand reachable), 531 admits a finite-state-counter automaton model.**

**Hypothesis (~60% confidence)**: 531 is decidable by a counter automaton. Decidability of non-halting for 1-counter machines is well-studied (Reversal-Bounded counter automata, etc.). If the right-side word set stabilizes to a finite reachable set, the problem reduces to checking whether the counter ever decreases to 0 (or reaches a halt configuration) from a reachable state - decidable.

Testing would require:
- Enumerate distinct right-side words across a much longer trace.
- Build the transition graph on `(w, N mod K)` pairs for small K.
- Look for halt-reachable states.

## What this DOES NOT show

- Neither word matches **Sturmian** (which would have c(n) = n+1 unbounded). This rules out the irrational-rotation hypothesis from `notes/10`.
- This is **observational**, not a proof. The complexity bounds we measured are over a finite trace; we can't yet rule out the complexity growing slowly at larger time scales.

## Why this matters

Combined with the shape-explosion result (`notes/10`), we now have a **two-axis taxonomy** for BB(3,3) holdouts:

| | Bigfoot | 153 | 397 | 531 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Macro shape recurrence | high (28.7% distinct) | high (31.5%) | none (100%) | almost none (95.8%) |
| Word factor complexity | ? (didn't measure) | ? | **bounded ≤ 22** | **bounded ≤ 21** |
| Word length | exp growth | poly growth | linear growth | **bounded (≤ 24)** |
| Quick_Sim rules | 2 | 16 | 0 | 0 |
| Candidate framework | counter triple (a,b,c) | shape-explosive, refuted | substitutive | counter automaton |

531 looks like the most tractable open holdout - the bounded right-side word is a *strong* structural hint.
